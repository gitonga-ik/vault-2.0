**Brain Tumor Segmentation and Classification from MRI Scans**
# 1. Project Definition

This project aims to develop a deep learning-based system for the  analysis of brain MRI scans, with two main goals: **brain tumor**  **segmentation** and **tumor classification**.

The segmentation part of the project will identify and outline the  region of the brain affected by a tumor. Instead of only determining  whether a tumor is present, the system will produce a segmentation mask  showing the location and boundaries of the tumor. This can also allow  measurements such as tumor size and volume to be calculated.

The second part of the project will focus on **classifying the**  **identified tumor** into its type, depending on the types of tumors  represented in the selected dataset. Possible classes may include  gliomas, meningiomas, and pituitary tumors, although the final classes  will depend on the availability and quality of suitable labelled MRI  data.

The project is intended as a research and decision-support system rather  than a replacement for radiologists, neurosurgeons, or oncologists.

# 2. Main Objectives

## 2.1 General Objective

To develop and evaluate a deep learning system that can segment brain  tumors in MRI scans and classify the detected tumors according to their  type.

## 2.2 Specific Objectives

1. To acquire and prepare a suitable publicly available brain MRI  dataset containing tumour images and appropriate labels or  segmentation masks.
    
2. To develop a preprocessing pipeline for preparing MRI scans for deep  learning, including steps such as normalization, resizing or  resampling, and data augmentation where appropriate.
    
3. To develop a deep learning image segmentation model capable of  identifying and delineating tumor regions within brain MRI scans.
    
4. To develop a classification model capable of predicting the type of  tumor from MRI information.
    
5. To investigate whether information obtained from the segmentation,  such as tumor shape, size, volume, and other image characteristics,  can be useful for tumor classification.
    
6. To evaluate the segmentation model using appropriate metrics such as  Dice Similarity Coefficient and Intersection over Union (IoU).
    
7. To evaluate the classification model using metrics such as accuracy,  precision, recall, F1-score, and a confusion matrix.
    
8. To develop a simple interface or visualization component that allows  the segmentation results to be viewed on MRI scans and the predicted  tumor class and measurements to be displayed.

# 3. What I Aim to Achieve

The main outcome of the project will be a working prototype that takes a  brain MRI scan as input and produces useful information about a possible  tumor.

A simplified version of the proposed workflow is:

              Brain MRI Scan  
                     |  
                     v  
              Preprocessing  
                     |  
                     v  
           +-------------------+  
           | Tumor Segmentation|  
           +-------------------+  
                     |                     v  
              Tumor Mask  
                     |  
           +---------+---------+  
           |                   |  
           v                   v  
     Tumor Measurements    Image Features  
     - Size               - Shape  
     - Volume             - Intensity  
     - Location           - Texture  
           |                   |  
           +---------+---------+  
                     |  
                     v  
           Tumor Classification  
                     |  
                     v  
         Predicted Tumor Type 

The final system should therefore be able to:

- Identify whether a tumor-like region is present.
    
- Locate and outline the tumor in the MRI.
    
- Produce a visual segmentation mask.
    
- Estimate characteristics such as tumor volume where the available  scan information supports this.
    
- Predict the tumor type supported by the training dataset.
    
- Display the results in a form that is easier to interpret.
    

The aim is not to claim that the system can independently diagnose a  patient. Instead, it will demonstrate how artificial intelligence can be  used to automate part of the medical image analysis process and provide  additional information that could assist qualified medical  professionals.

**4. Possible Data Sources**

The project will rely on publicly available datasets rather than  collecting patient MRI scans directly. This makes the project more  realistic within the available time and avoids the additional  difficulties involved in collecting and manually annotating clinical  data.

**4.1 BraTS (Brain Tumor Segmentation Challenge)**

BraTS is a major source to consider for the segmentation part of the  project. It provides brain MRI data together with expert-generated tumor  segmentation labels.

The dataset is particularly useful because it supports research into  identifying the location and different regions of brain tumors. It  commonly includes multiple MRI sequences, such as T1, T1-contrast, T2,  and FLAIR.

BraTS would therefore be a strong candidate for training and evaluating  the segmentation component.

**4.2 The Cancer Imaging Archive (TCIA)**

The Cancer Imaging Archive provides access to large collections of  medical imaging data for research. It may be useful when looking for  additional MRI datasets and clinical information.

The suitability of a particular collection will depend on whether it  contains the tumor labels and segmentation annotations required for this  project.

**4.3 Kaggle and Other Research Datasets**

Kaggle contains several brain MRI datasets that can be useful for  initial experimentation, particularly for developing a basic prototype.

However, the final dataset should preferably contain reliable labels  and, for the segmentation component, expert-generated segmentation  masks. Dataset quality will therefore be considered before selecting the  final training and testing data.

**5. Proposed Technology Stack**

**Programming Language**

**Python**

Python will be the main programming language because it has a large  ecosystem of libraries for machine learning, deep learning, medical  image processing, and data analysis.

**Deep Learning**

**PyTorch**

PyTorch will be used to develop and train the deep learning models. It  provides flexibility for experimenting with different architectures and  training approaches.

**Medical Imaging / Segmentation**

**MONAI (Medical Open Network for AI)**

MONAI will be considered for the medical imaging pipeline, particularly  for working with 3D MRI data, preprocessing, data loading, augmentation,  and medical image segmentation models.

**Segmentation Models**

The initial segmentation model will likely be based on the **U-Net**  **architecture**, since U-Net is widely used for medical image  segmentation.

Depending on the results and available computational resources, more  advanced architectures can be investigated as a comparison or  improvement.

**MRI / NIfTI Processing**

Libraries such as **NiBabel** will be used where necessary to read and  work with NIfTI MRI files (.nii / .nii.gz).

**Data Analysis**

The project may use:

- NumPy for numerical operations
    
- Pandas for structured data
    
- Matplotlib for visualization
    
- Scikit-learn for classification metrics and supporting  machine-learning tasks
    

**Development and Visualization**

A simple interface could be developed using **Streamlit** or **Gradio**.  This would allow a user to upload or select an MRI scan, view the  segmentation overlay, and see the predicted tumor type and extracted  measurements.

**6. Why This Project Is Important**

Brain tumors can require complex diagnosis and treatment planning. MRI  scans contain a large amount of information, but analysing and  interpreting these scans requires specialized medical expertise.

One of the potential problems is the amount of time required to manually  identify and delineate tumor regions. A system that can automatically  highlight a suspected tumor and provide quantitative information could  help reduce some of the repetitive image-analysis work.

This project could therefore have potential value as a  **decision-support tool for neurosurgeons, oncologists, and**  **radiologists**. For example, an automated segmentation could provide a  visual representation of the tumor and measurements such as its  approximate volume, which could then be reviewed by a qualified  professional.

The broader social value of the project would be in investigating  whether AI can make medical image analysis more efficient and  consistent, particularly in settings where medical specialists may have  large numbers of scans to review.

However, the project will not claim to replace medical professionals.  The system will be developed and evaluated as a research prototype, and  any real clinical use would require much more extensive validation,  regulatory approval, and testing with clinical data.

**7. Proposed Scope**

To keep the project achievable within approximately six months, the  initial scope will focus exclusively on **brain MRI scans**.

The project will concentrate on two main computer vision tasks:

1. **Segmentation** -- identifying and delineating the tumor region in  an MRI scan.
    
2. **Classification** -- determining the tumor type represented by the  available labelled data.
    

The project will initially avoid incorporating CT scans or other imaging  modalities. This keeps the data requirements and preprocessing pipeline  more manageable and allows greater focus on developing and evaluating  the MRI-based system.

The exact tumor classes and whether the classification will be performed  directly from the MRI or from features derived from the segmentation  will be finalized after reviewing the available datasets.

**8. Expected Final Deliverable**

The expected deliverable will be a prototype medical image analysis  system consisting of:

- A dataset preparation and preprocessing pipeline.
    
- A trained MRI tumor segmentation model.
    
- A trained tumor classification model.
    
- Quantitative evaluation of both models.
    
- Visualization of MRI scans with tumor segmentation overlays.
    
- Basic tumor measurements such as estimated volume where appropriate.
    
- A simple interface for demonstrating the system.
    
- A report documenting the methodology, experiments, results,  limitations, and possible future improvements.
    

Overall, the project will investigate how deep learning and image  segmentation can be applied to brain MRI analysis to automatically  locate tumors and classify them, while providing quantitative and visual  information that could potentially support medical professionals in  their work.
